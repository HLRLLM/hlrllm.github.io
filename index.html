Hear, Localize, and Reason:
Spatially Aware Scene Understanding for Audio-Visual LLMs

NeurIPS 2026
Kim Sung-Bin1, Lee Jung-Mok2, Jinwoo Jung2, Oh Hyun-Bin1,
Hyeonggon Ryu3, David Harwath4, Tae-Hyun Oh2
1POSTECH 2KAIST 3Hankuk University of Foreign Studies 4The University of Texas at Austin
arXiv Paper Code HLR-AVSceneQA

HLR-AVSceneQA is a comprehensive benchmark for spatial audio-visual scene understanding, organized around a hear–localize–reason hierarchy. For a queried sound source, the model recognizes what is heard, localizes where it originates in egocentric and allocentric views, and uses the localized source together with visual scene evidence to reason about how it relates to the surrounding scene.

Abstract

Audio-visual large language models (AV-LLMs) have made strong progress in multimodal understanding, yet most treat audio as monaural semantic content, limiting their ability to reason about where sounds originate and how they relate to the visual scene. Recent spatial audio-visual studies address parts of this problem, but often focus on specific abilities, such as spatial correspondence or direction and distance reasoning. In this work, we introduce HLR-AVSceneQA, a comprehensive benchmark with a hear-localize-reason hierarchy that evaluates whether models can recognize what is heard, localize sound sources in egocentric and allocentric views, and use these localized sources to reason about how they relate to the visual scene. We further introduce HLR-LLM, which augments a strong audio-visual foundation model with a binaural spatial audio branch alongside its monaural semantic audio pathway. We train HLR-LLM with a curriculum that incorporates final-stage chain-of-thought supervision for multi-step spatial reasoning. Experiments show that existing AV-LLMs remain limited in spatial grounding and relational reasoning, while HLR-LLM substantially improves spatial audio-visual scene understanding without compromising semantic audio-visual perception.

Contributions

  • We introduce HLR-AVSceneQA, a comprehensive benchmark that evaluates spatial hearing and grounding both directly and as intermediate evidence for downstream spatial relational reasoning.
  • We propose HLR-LLM, a spatially aware AV-LLM that augments a strong foundation model with a spatial audio pathway to improve grounding and reasoning while preserving semantic perception.
  • We demonstrate that final-stage chain-of-thought curriculum training improves multi-step spatial reasoning by connecting auditory, spatial, and visual cues through intermediate reasoning steps.

HLR-AVSceneQA includes 53,279 training and 4,869 test pairs from scenes with multiple simultaneously active sound sources. Because collecting real-world spatial audio-visual data with accurate annotations is challenging, we construct the benchmark with a two-stage simulator-based pipeline: Stage I synthesizes spatial audio-visual scenes with structured metadata, and Stage II generates QA pairs from this metadata. Unlike panoramic-input settings, HLR-AVSceneQA uses normal-field-of-view egocentric observations, requiring models to reason about visible, occluded, and off-screen sources from partial visual evidence.

Stage I — Spatial audio-visual scene synthesis

  • Scene and acoustic simulator. SoundSpaces 2.0 renders geometry-aware room acoustics and spatializes dry monaural clips into binaural audio. 3D environments come from HM3DSem (216 indoor spaces, 3,100 rooms with dense semantics); 145 scenes for training and 36 for testing.
  • Audio source curation. Non-speech sounds from VGGSound (310+ classes), manually filtered to remove classes that are not naturally grounded to a single indoor object, plus ImageBind-based text-audio similarity filtering of likely mislabeled clips. Speech comes from LibriSpeech for far-field ASR.
  • Visual object curation. A 3D mesh pool of sounding objects from Objaverse-XL, supplemented with off-the-shelf 2D/3D generation models for under-covered categories — roughly 81 meshes per category for training and 35 for testing, all manually checked.
  • Scene assembly. 2–6 candidate meshes are inserted per room and up to two are activated as sound sources. Some audio-object pairs are semantically matched, others are spatially aligned but semantically mismatched. Sources may be fully visible, occluded, in an adjacent room, or outside the field of view. The final RGB image is rendered at 1280×720 with a 90° horizontal field of view, paired with binaural audio.

Stage II — Task design and QA construction

1  Spatial hearing

Sound event recognition — identify audible event categories.  ·  Far-field ASR — transcribe spoken content from far-field audio.

2  Spatial grounding

Egocentric direction / distance — localize the source relative to the agent's current viewpoint.  ·  Allocentric direction / distance — localize it from a transformed viewpoint, defined by standing at a reference object and facing another object.

3  Spatial relational reasoning

Visibility — is the source visible from the current view?  ·  Correspondence — which visible object is spatially aligned with the sound (the answer may be semantically mismatched, or None)?  ·  Hidden-source localization — is a non-visible source hidden behind an object, in an adjacent room, or outside the field of view?  ·  Loudness change — does the source get louder or quieter after the agent moves to a target object?  ·  Proximity — which candidate object is nearest to the source?  ·  Scene captioning — summarize sound events, directions, and visibility states.
Chain-of-thought supervision example
Q: Given the sound, where is its source located?
A: The sound event is a hair dryer drying, arriving from the front-left direction and 2.7 meters away. The hair dryer is not visible because it is occluded by the armchair. Therefore, the source is hidden behind the armchair.
CoT rationales are deterministically generated from structured simulator metadata using task-specific templates; GPT-4 is used only to paraphrase question wording.

HLR-LLM

Architecture and training curriculum of HLR-LLM. (a) HLR-LLM augments a strong AV-LLM with a binaural spatial audio branch, enabling the language model to jointly attend to text, visual, downmixed monaural audio, and binaural spatial audio tokens. (b) The model is trained through a three-stage curriculum: spatial audio alignment, spatial audio-visual instruction tuning, and final-stage CoT tuning for structured spatial reasoning.

HLR-LLM is built on Qwen2.5-Omni and adds a spatial audio pathway alongside the existing visual and monaural audio branches. A binaural signal is encoded by a frozen spatial audio encoder and mapped into the language model embedding space by a trainable MLP projector. A new media token, <spatial_audio>, marks the placeholder positions that are replaced with the projected spatial audio tokens, giving the input sequence X = [E(q), Zvis, Zaud, Zspa]. The monaural branch preserves semantic hearing ability, while the spatial branch supplies direction- and distance-sensitive cues for grounding and reasoning.

Three-stage curriculum

Stage Training data → Objective # Samples Trainable
1 Spatial audio-only QA → Spatial audio alignment 514k Spatial projector
2 HLR-AVSceneQA → Spatial audio-visual instruction tuning 175k Spatial projector + LoRA
3 HLR-AVSceneQA-CoT → Chain-of-thought tuning 94.5k Spatial projector + LoRA

All visual, monaural audio, and spatial audio encoders remain frozen throughout training. LoRA uses rank 16, alpha 16, dropout 0.05, learning rate 2×10−4, and weight decay 0.01; 2 epochs on four NVIDIA A6000 GPUs with a per-device batch size of 4. Binaural audio is sampled at 32 kHz with a 1024 window, 320 hop, and 128 mel bins.

Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples of spatial grounding. In the left example, multiple visually similar electric blenders are present while the sound originates from the front-right direction. The monaural Qwen2.5-Omni predicts an incorrect direction, whereas models with binaural spatial input correctly localize the source. The right example is more challenging because the sounding bird is not visible and must be localized using spatial audio and scene layout: Qwen2.5-Omni and Hear You Are LLM attribute the source to other non-visible regions, while HLR-LLM correctly identifies it as hidden behind the bed.

Limitations

HLR-AVSceneQA is built with a simulator-based pipeline, which provides controllable annotations but cannot fully capture the acoustic complexity and visual diversity of the real world. The benchmark focuses on indoor scenes, leaving dynamic agents, moving sound sources, and richer embodied interaction for future work. Future research should extend spatial audio-visual benchmarks to more realistic and dynamic settings and develop AV-LLMs that can actively perceive, move, and reason in spatial environments.
